Seized aluminum seatpost in a Columbus SL frame — how do I get it out without wrecking the paint?

BCBen Carver Asked 3 hours ago1,204 viewsFollowing: 38

Picked up a 1984 frame with original paint and decals. The Campagnolo post hasn't moved in what I'm guessing is 20+ years. I've soaked it with penetrating oil from the top and through the bottom bracket shell for a week and tried twisting with the saddle on — nothing.

I've read about cutting the post and sawing it out from the inside, and also about an ammonia soak to dissolve the corrosion. Is ammonia safe for steel tubing and the paint? Anyone here done this on a frame they cared about?

I've pulled about forty of these out of lugged steel. Ammonia is the right call for an aluminum post in a steel frame — it attacks the aluminum oxide, not the steel or the paint, as long as you keep it off the outside of the frame.

  1. Strip the frame bare and plug the bottom bracket shell from the inside with a rubber stopper.
  2. Flip the frame upside down, seat tube vertical, and fill through the BB shell with household ammonia until it covers the post's insertion depth.
  3. Wrap the seat cluster in plastic and leave it 48–72 hours. You'll see grey sludge — that's the corrosion lifting.
  4. Drain, rinse with water, then clamp the post (not the frame) in a bench vise with soft jaws and rotate the frame.
  5. Flush the tube and fog it with frame saver before reinstalling anything.

If it still won't budge after two rounds, message me before you cut anything. I keep a reamer set for 26.8 and 27.0 that I lend out locally.
